# JP Match Art Direction — Pre-selection Brief

Status: **Direction C — ことば結び selected.** Visual-system contract approved for implementation.

## 1. Product and player experience

JP Match teaches recognition by asking the player to connect two forms of the same Japanese sound or meaning: romaji, hiragana, katakana, pictures, and spoken audio. Its emotional rhythm is curiosity → recall → confirmation → collection. In two-player mode, that learning loop also becomes a light face-to-face contest.

Mechanically critical information:

- which player acts now;
- which two cards are currently revealed;
- whether the pair matched;
- which representation each face uses;
- both scores and the number of pairs remaining;
- whether input is temporarily locked;
- what was pronounced.

## 2. Current visual diagnosis

### Most damaging issues

1. **The setup screen reads as a styled web form.** Repeated pills, full-width selects, equal spacing, and a centered column organize configuration cleanly but do not communicate memory, language, or matching. Structurally redesign it around the player’s first meaningful choice.
2. **The gameplay board is visually consistent but conceptually generic.** The seal, corner brackets, gradients, glow, and diagonal texture decorate every card, yet they do not distinguish sound, script, pair relationships, or learning progress. Visually integrate card construction with the selected metaphor.
3. **Matched cards vanish into unexplained holes.** The holes report removal but not knowledge gained, pair identity, or player ownership. Replace the absence with a progression trace tied to the chosen direction.

### Delete rather than polish

- background stripes that only fill space;
- continuous glow and pulsing where a static structural cue would be clearer;
- pill geometry applied to nearly every control;
- decorative card rings and corner marks unless they gain a mechanic-specific meaning;
- equal visual weight across all setup fields.

### Retain and build upon

- the direct two-card memory loop;
- fast card flip timing and audible pronunciation;
- full-board use of the screen;
- visible left/right player opposition;
- three content themes only where they become coherent variants of one selected system;
- the clear distinction between kana, romaji, pictures, and calendar symbols;
- reduced-motion support.

### Asset reality

- 269 local MP3 files cover kana, vocabulary, sound effects, and three BGM tracks.
- 26 custom home-object PNG stickers use a soft dimensional illustration style.
- Other vocabulary relies on platform emoji, so a final direction must either normalize that difference or deliberately frame the two asset families.

## 3. Direction A — 校對桌 / The Proofreader’s Table

Category: restrained and editorial.

1. **Design thesis:** JP Match behaves like a working Japanese proof sheet where two notations are compared, corrected, and approved.
2. **Player emotion:** quiet concentration, precision, and the satisfaction of making a correct editorial mark.
3. **Visual metaphor:** a typographer’s proof, marginal annotations, registration marks, and correction slips.
4. **Signature element:** a single vermilion correction stroke physically links a correct pair, then migrates into a narrow “approved pairs” margin.
5. **Dominant compositional gesture:** a wide proof sheet with an off-center vertical annotation rail; the board occupies the page, while score and remaining count live in the margin.
6. **Deliberate asymmetry:** the active player owns either the left or right proof margin; the center is reserved for learning content.
7. **Typography behavior:** large Mincho kana as specimens, sober grotesk romaji and controls, tabular numerals, sparse Traditional Chinese notes.
8. **Shape rules:** squared paper slips, cropped rules, open margins; very limited corner rounding; controls grouped by editorial hierarchy rather than boxes.
9. **Material rules:** warm uncoated paper, black ink, graphite, and one vermilion correction ink; no glass or luminous surfaces.
10. **Motion behavior:** cards set down, correction marks draw once, and approved pairs slide into the margin. Primary verb: typeset; supporting verbs: underline and stamp.
11. **Relationship to gameplay:** the core act is comparison between two written forms; success becomes a visible editorial approval rather than a generic burst.
12. **Risks and tradeoffs:** can feel academic or severe; picture vocabulary and young learners need enough warmth and scale.
13. **Why it belongs to JP Match:** its hierarchy is built from comparing Japanese scripts and collecting verified equivalences, not from a generic paper theme.

## 4. Direction B — 障子記憶屋敷 / The Memory House

Category: diegetic and world-integrated.

1. **Design thesis:** each game opens a quiet house of sliding panels; every match reveals a shared object, sound, or view behind two connected openings.
2. **Player emotion:** curiosity, intimacy, and the pleasure of gradually opening a place.
3. **Visual metaphor:** shoji partitions, a tokonoma display space, room thresholds, and changing daylight.
4. **Signature element:** a matched pair slides open as two coordinated shutters and reveals one continuous illustrated vignette spanning both positions.
5. **Dominant compositional gesture:** a framed wall of panels with a low horizontal floor line; setup is a route through rooms rather than a form column.
6. **Deliberate asymmetry:** a tokonoma-like side bay holds mode, remaining pairs, and the collected match of the moment.
7. **Typography behavior:** labels sit on small wooden or paper nameplates; learning glyphs remain large and undecorated inside the openings.
8. **Shape rules:** rectilinear panel divisions, sliding tracks, deep thresholds; circles appear only for finger pulls or sound sources.
9. **Material rules:** paper fiber, pale wood, cloth bindings, daylight and shadow used as spatial evidence—not glass blur or glow.
10. **Motion behavior:** slide, overlap, and reveal. A mismatch closes softly; a turn change shifts the light and floor-side ownership.
11. **Relationship to gameplay:** memory is spatial; matched knowledge literally opens the shared environment while unmatched panels close again.
12. **Risks and tradeoffs:** art requirements are higher, dense 10×5 boards can weaken the architectural illusion, and Japanese-house imagery must avoid tourist cliché.
13. **Why it belongs to JP Match:** each pair transforms two language panels into one learned scene, joining spatial memory with Japanese recognition.

## 5. Direction C — ことば結び / The Pairing Cord

Category: mechanic-driven.

1. **Design thesis:** the entire interface visualizes invisible relationships becoming explicit connections.
2. **Player emotion:** anticipation, pattern discovery, and competitive momentum.
3. **Visual metaphor:** labeled study tags joined by braided cords and collected at opposite player spindles.
4. **Signature element:** a correct pair pulls a taut cord between its two board positions; the cord then winds toward the scoring player without erasing the pair’s history.
5. **Dominant compositional gesture:** a broad central field crossed by a small number of meaningful diagonals, anchored by player spindles at opposite edges.
6. **Deliberate asymmetry:** revealed cards tug toward the active player’s edge, giving turn ownership a spatial force rather than merely a color.
7. **Typography behavior:** kana and romaji are printed on differently cut tags; pair identity is learned through type, cut, and orientation together.
8. **Shape rules:** tag notches encode representation side; open anchors replace uniform card frames; knots only indicate confirmed matches.
9. **Material rules:** fibrous tags, ink, and woven cord; no glossy panels. Player distinction uses cord weave and endpoint shape in addition to hue.
10. **Motion behavior:** tug, tension, and wind. Mismatch releases tension and recoils briefly; reduced motion replaces travel with a drawn static connection.
11. **Relationship to gameplay:** matching is not represented after the fact—the formation of a connection is the main visual event and the persistent progress map.
12. **Risks and tradeoffs:** crossed cords can become noisy on large boards; the system needs strict routing, fading, and maximum-visible-connection rules.
13. **Why it belongs to JP Match:** the UI is generated from the exact equivalence relation between two Japanese representations and from two-player ownership.

## 6. Direction D — 折り記憶布 / The Folding Memory Cloth

Category: high-risk and experimental.

1. **Design thesis:** the board is one continuous folded surface; individual “cards” are creases that expose paired fragments when lifted.
2. **Player emotion:** tactile surprise, playful risk, and the satisfaction of resolving a complex surface.
3. **Visual metaphor:** a furoshiki-like learning cloth folded into pockets, with information hidden under alternating flaps.
4. **Signature element:** every match refolds two distant flaps into one bold shared shape, gradually changing the board’s silhouette instead of leaving rectangular holes.
5. **Dominant compositional gesture:** a diagonal fold structure that breaks the orthogonal grid while preserving predictable touch regions.
6. **Deliberate asymmetry:** a large master fold travels from the active player’s corner toward the opponent, making competitive momentum visible.
7. **Typography behavior:** characters align to fold axes and reorient on reveal; body labels stay level at the edge for readability.
8. **Shape rules:** clipped polygons, fold triangles, and continuous seams; rounded rectangles are absent; every edge must belong to the cloth geometry.
9. **Material rules:** matte woven color fields, printed ink, stitched edge codes, and restrained shadow only where a real fold changes depth.
10. **Motion behavior:** lift, unfold, and refold. Success resolves two folds into one stable patch; mismatch returns the creases to their prior tension.
11. **Relationship to gameplay:** memorization becomes knowledge of a changing surface, while matched pairs physically simplify and reshape it.
12. **Risks and tradeoffs:** highest implementation and accessibility risk; large boards, keyboard focus, text orientation, and reduced motion require early prototypes.
13. **Why it belongs to JP Match:** the folding logic expresses two fragments resolving into one learned equivalence and makes every completed board a unique record of play.

## 7. Recommendation

Recommended starting point: **Direction C — ことば結び**.

It expresses the exact matching mechanic, gives successful pairs a persistent and meaningful trace, strengthens two-player spatial ownership, and can reuse the existing content and custom stickers without demanding a full environment art set. Direction A is the safest alternative if maximum classroom clarity and lower implementation risk are the priority.

## 8. Selected visual-system contract

### Design thesis

JP Match turns hidden equivalences into visible connections: two representations become one learned pair, and every successful connection travels toward the player who found it.

### Required shape rules

1. Learning cards use clipped study-tag silhouettes with one structural notch, never a generic rounded rectangle.
2. Player 1 uses solid lines and diamond endpoints; Player 2 uses broken lines and square endpoints, so ownership survives grayscale.
3. Controls use open rules, cut corners, and grouped baselines; boxes appear only when an interactive boundary is necessary.

### Forbidden shape rules

1. No pill geometry for every option, score, or status.
2. No circular container unless it represents a spool, knot, or sound source.
3. No uniform framed panels that give setup, gameplay, and result equal visual weight.

### Signature gesture

A successful pair leaves two knot anchors in its former card positions. A taut connection joins them and a tail routes toward the scoring player’s edge. The newest connection is strongest; older connections remain as a restrained progress map.

### Deliberate asymmetry

The active player visually pulls the board toward their edge. Player 1 owns the left spool; Player 2 owns the right spool. Setup uses an offset title block and an editorial configuration field rather than one centered stack.

### Typography behavior

- Kana: Shippori Mincho, largest learning form, upright and spacious.
- Romaji: Zen Kaku Gothic New, lowercase, compact, mechanically precise.
- Traditional Chinese: concise interface guidance in the body face.
- Scores and remaining pairs: tabular-width numerals with visible labels.
- Representation differences must remain legible without relying on font family alone; tag notch and small side mark support typography.

## 9. Semantic tokens

### Color roles

- world/background: deep woven indigo, mist blue-gray, or warm yuzu cloth depending on theme;
- learning tag: quiet paper with strong dark ink;
- interactive rule: theme-appropriate high-contrast cord;
- Player 1: cool cord plus diamond endpoint;
- Player 2: warm cord plus square endpoint;
- success: knowledge-green knot, used briefly before ownership styling;
- failure: broken red-brown stitch plus a text/shape change;
- warning: high-contrast accent reserved for invalid setup or urgency;
- disabled: low-contrast surface plus reduced opacity and disabled semantics.

### Type scale

- display/title: `clamp(2.6rem, 7vw, 5.4rem)`;
- section/turn: `1rem–1.25rem` with strong weight;
- body/control: `0.92rem–1rem`;
- metadata: `0.72rem–0.82rem` with generous tracking;
- card content: container-relative, preserving current density behavior.

### Spacing and surfaces

- base rhythm: 4px; primary intervals: 8, 12, 20, 32px;
- screen margins remain compact during gameplay and more generous during setup;
- setup has one compositional field, not a stack of cards;
- cards sit above cords; matched anchors expose cords beneath them;
- shadows express tag lift only and must not become ambient glow.

### Border and corner logic

- cards: clipped tag polygon with 2–4px corner softness only where clipping would alias;
- setup controls: one-pixel rules and cut trailing corner;
- start action: strong diagonal cut, no rounded rectangle;
- modal/result: asymmetric sheet or tray with one clipped corner.

### Icon rules

- use text labels for ambiguous actions;
- custom home stickers remain unaltered inside neutral tag fields;
- emoji vocabulary receives the same tag, scale, and underlay treatment as custom images;
- endpoint shapes and line patterns act as functional icons for player ownership.

## 10. Motion language

Primary verb: **pull**. Supporting verbs: **tension** and **wind**.

Duration tiers:

- instant feedback: 90–120ms;
- local reveal or selection: 220–300ms;
- match connection or turn transfer: 420–650ms;
- result composition: up to 900ms, without delaying input.

Motion rules:

- entrance: title thread draws toward the setup controls; controls themselves remain still;
- focus: a short cord segment appears beside the focused element, without scaling the whole control;
- selection: the chosen tag shifts 2px toward its anchor in 120ms;
- card reveal: retain the existing 280ms flip, restyled as a tag turning over;
- success: both tags settle, knot anchors appear, then the cord draws from the pair midpoint toward the scoring edge;
- mismatch: the exposed tags show a broken stitch and release tension; no shake or red flash wall;
- turn change: the active edge spindle advances while the previous side relaxes;
- interruption: restarting clears all cords immediately before a new board appears;
- reduced motion: reveal becomes a near-instant face swap; connections appear fully drawn with no travel; ownership remains visible through pattern and endpoint shape.

Maximum simultaneous effects: one card reveal sequence plus one ownership/connection event. No ambient card, score, arrow, and edge pulsing at the same time.

## 11. Component map and implementation brief

### Setup

- `brand`: offset title, short learning promise, decorative but mechanic-related thread path;
- `setup-panel`: two-column configuration field on wide screens, one column on phones;
- `opt`: cut-corner choice tag with a visible anchor mark;
- `setup-select` and `range-select`: ruled selectors with restrained chevron;
- `btn-start`: diagonal “pull tab” action.

### Gameplay

- `game-top`: compact utility row;
- `turn-banner`: active spool label, using player-specific endpoint icon and pattern;
- `score-pill`: renamed visually as player spindle; existing DOM/class retained for compatibility;
- `board`: grid plus an absolute SVG cord layer;
- `card`: clipped study tag; content side encoded after reveal;
- `match-anchor`: persistent knot marker in completed positions;
- `pair-cord`: SVG connection and ownership tail generated from matched card geometry.

### Result and menu

- result: accumulated cord gesture becomes the scoreboard divider;
- menu: asymmetric settings tray, no glass blur, stable focus order and labels;
- theme controls remain available but restyled as thread samples rather than pills.

### State and implementation requirements

- add `matchTraces` to game state;
- create and redraw the SVG cord layer after board render, match, and viewport resize;
- preserve the existing card deck, matching rules, timers, audio calls, and result logic;
- card anchors must not intercept input;
- resize drawing must be requestAnimationFrame-throttled;
- keep all critical text and button accessible names intact;
- make turn banner a polite live region;
- ensure focus visibility and ownership patterns survive all three themes.

## 12. Responsive behavior

- `>900px`: setup title and configuration occupy offset columns; gameplay retains a wide central field with edge spindles.
- `701–900px`: setup returns to one centered composition; board and HUD remain horizontal where possible.
- `≤700px portrait`: board transposes as today; cords redraw to the transposed geometry; player spindles remain side-by-side above it.
- `≤390px` or short height: metadata contracts before card content; setup scrolls; start action remains reachable.
- Cord stroke widths and anchor sizes scale with viewport but stay visually quieter than revealed card text.

## 13. Accessibility constraints

- Player ownership uses position, line pattern, endpoint geometry, and text in addition to color.
- Matched anchors include a persistent visual symbol; screen readers continue to receive updated score and remaining text.
- Focus outlines must be at least 2px and offset outside clipped tag shapes.
- Touch targets remain at least 44px outside the densest board presets; dense cards retain full-cell hit targets.
- No critical information depends on the 280ms match hold or 900ms mismatch hold.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` removes line drawing, pulsing, and large transforms while retaining state changes.
- Custom images keep empty alt text because the button’s accessible label supplies the learning meaning.

## 14. Screenshot acceptance criteria

- Setup does not resemble a dashboard or a centered stack of pills.
- A cropped gameplay image shows tag silhouettes, edge spindles, and at least one meaningful pair connection.
- Player 1 and Player 2 remain distinguishable in grayscale.
- Matched positions communicate pair history rather than blank absence.
- Kana and romaji remain readable at `390x667` and `1280x720`.
- No clipping occurs in setup, menu, result, or 10×5 gameplay.
- Reduced-motion mode retains all critical visual state.
- No severity-1 or severity-2 issue remains after two screenshot-based review cycles.
